# CRE Platform — Human Editorial Production System

Status: M0.4 v0.1; validate with CRE-015 before final freeze.

## Editorial north star
Create CRE content that a working analyst, underwriter, lender, investor, broker, appraiser or serious learner would consider useful, credible and worth returning to.

Important pages should deliver one or more of: understanding, application, judgment and connection. Flagships should deliver all four.

## Voice
Professional, practitioner-oriented, clear, calm, analytical and conversational without becoming casual. Write like a knowledgeable colleague explaining a deal at a desk, not a textbook, marketing agency, influencer or chatbot.

## Explain the decision, not only the definition
Move through: What is it? → How is it calculated? → Where does the information come from? → How is it used? → What is the analyst trying to determine? → What could distort the answer? → What happens if the assumption changes? → How does it affect the deal?

## Depth levels
### Foundation
Assume intelligence but not prior CRE knowledge. Explain terminology naturally, use simple examples and establish the base concept before exceptions.

### Practitioner
Assume basic CRE vocabulary. Emphasize source documents, calculations, adjustments, assumptions, reconciliation, interpretation, red flags and workflow.

### Institutional
Emphasize nuance, evidence, differing practices, program context and defensible judgment. Explain what “it depends” depends on.

## AI-pattern blacklist
Avoid formulaic openings, filler transitions, mechanical section templates, repetitive paragraph lengths, forced three-item lists, keyword stuffing and generic conclusions that merely repeat the introduction. Individual words are not banned; repetitive patterns are.

## No fake experience
Never invent first-person deal experience, committee participation or borrower interactions. Human contributors may use genuine experience only when they supplied and approved the attribution.

## Facts versus judgment
Distinguish:
- externally verifiable fact
- calculation
- market convention
- program requirement
- editorial interpretation
- practitioner judgment

## Source hierarchy
1. Official/public primary sources
2. Government, regulatory and institutional sources
3. Recognized professional/industry research
4. Credible specialist secondary sources
5. Practitioner commentary

Search summaries, anonymous blogs and AI answers are not sources of record.

## Agency rule
Program-specific Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac claims must be checked against current official public sources and carry appropriate program/context, source/update date and review date. Official branding does not make a restricted document reusable. Do not use confidential, employer-restricted or proprietary materials.

## Formula standard
For major formulas provide, as appropriate: formula → variable definitions → units → worked example → interpretation → limitations. Teach what drives the inputs, not just arithmetic.

## Example policy
Examples must be original and fictional. Never reconstruct employer/client deals or lightly alter confidential numbers. Reusable fictional properties may support multiple lessons if clearly identified as educational cases.

## Judgment and uncertainty
Do not normalize or adjust a number merely because it changed. Investigate why it changed. Where multiple assumptions may be defensible, show the evidence and competing interpretations.

## “So what?” test
After important analysis, explain why the finding matters to cash flow, value, coverage, leverage, loan sizing, returns or credit risk.

## SEO rule
Research and editorial quality precede SEO. SEO may improve titles, descriptions, headings, terminology coverage, internal links and structured data; it must not dictate the argument.

## Source-note rule
For serious content, create source notes and a fact/claim map before drafting. Capture the fact, supported claim, scope/qualification, date sensitivity and potential conflicts.

## Canonical production pipeline
1. Question / user intent
2. Research plan
3. Source collection
4. Source notes
5. Fact & claim map
6. Article brief
7. First draft
8. CRE practitioner pass
9. Human editorial pass
10. Fact + source verification
11. Example / calculation check
12. Internal linking
13. SEO pass
14. Read-aloud pass
15. Publication scorecard
16. Approved

The intensity may be reduced for simple glossary content. Flagships and program-specific content use the full process.

## Practitioner pass
Ask whether the piece explains actual analysis, source documents, calculations versus judgment, adjustments, material exceptions, broader deal effects and something genuinely useful to a working CRE reader.

## Human editorial pass
Check naturalness, paragraph/section variation, mechanical transitions, excessive headings, filler, slow introductions, repetitive conclusions and removable prose. If substantial text can be deleted without losing meaning, tighten it.

## Read-aloud test
Professional writing should sound natural when spoken by a knowledgeable person explaining the subject.

## Publication scorecard
| Dimension | Points |
|---|---:|
| Technical CRE accuracy | 20 |
| Source quality & fidelity | 15 |
| Practitioner usefulness | 15 |
| Depth & insight | 10 |
| Clarity | 10 |
| Human editorial quality | 10 |
| Examples/calculations | 5 |
| Knowledge connections/internal links | 5 |
| Search intent satisfaction | 5 |
| Originality/non-redundancy | 5 |
| Total | 100 |

90–100: publish. 85–89: minor revision. 75–84: substantive revision. Below 75: do not publish. A serious technical-accuracy or source-fidelity failure is an automatic fail. Flagships target 95+.

## Guest contributors
Contributor Draft → Editorial Review → Technical Review → Source Check → Contributor Approval of Material Edits → Publish. Preserve a contributor's authentic voice rather than rewriting every contributor into one house template.

## AI assistance
AI assistance does not remove human editorial responsibility. Research, claims, calculations and final copy must meet the same standard regardless of who or what produced the first draft.

## Editorial freeze
Once content has status `approved` or `published`, substantive editorial copy is protected. Engineering work may render, style, index and link it but must not rewrite it unless the task explicitly authorizes editorial modification.

## Benchmark
CRE-015 — Net Operating Income: From Property Financials to Underwritten NOI — is the first benchmark used to validate this system. After the benchmark exposes any weaknesses, revise this standard and freeze M0.4.
