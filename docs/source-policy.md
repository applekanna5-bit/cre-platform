# Source Policy

## Source selection

Public authoritative sources are preferred for institutional, regulatory, and program-specific factual claims. Source provenance—including title, publisher, source type, relevant dates, URL when applicable, and review context—must be retained so a claim can be traced and reassessed.

Program-specific guidance, including any future Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac material, must ultimately be checked against current official public sources. Facts that can change over time must have suitable accessed or reviewed dates and an expectation for periodic review or prompt review when the underlying guidance changes.

Secondary industry and academic sources may add context or analysis, but they do not replace an available primary authority for a claim about an official program or requirement. Editorial interpretation must be distinguishable from sourced fact.

## Prohibited and unavailable material

Employer-confidential, client-confidential, proprietary, restricted, or otherwise unauthorized material must not enter the repository. This includes confidential examples and reproductions or close derivatives of restricted templates.

The project must not depend on inaccessible Claude outputs. If Claude-generated files ever become legally and safely available, they may serve only as optional secondary reference material and never as the source of truth.

Original templates must be developed independently from public information, first-principles analysis, and authorized original work rather than by reproducing restricted employer or client templates.

## Stewardship

Authors and reviewers must preserve source fidelity, record provenance, identify uncertainty, and revisit time-sensitive claims. A source record supports verification; it does not by itself establish that every statement drawn from the source is current or correctly interpreted.
