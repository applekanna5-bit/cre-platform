A one-page loan request can be enough to start a conversation. It is rarely enough to price the risk. The first pass should answer a narrower question: is this a plausible request for this lender or program, and what must be verified before anyone relies on the proposed terms?

## Put the request in one sentence

A fictional buyer wants $6.0 million of acquisition debt for a 60-unit apartment property and says it can close in 45 days. Its purchase contract calls for $9.0 million. Estimated costs and initial funding needs add $300,000. Total uses are $9.3 million, so a $6.0 million loan needs $3.3 million of equity. Establish whether that equity is committed, when it is available and whether any of it comes from another borrowing. A call with the buyer can resolve ambiguity about the intended use of proceeds before the analyst starts sizing.

Identify the proposed borrower, its controlling parties and the sponsor. Ask about similar properties operated, who manages this one, relevant financial capacity, liquidity after closing and whether any guarantee is contemplated. Check the lender's actual eligibility policy and the program's applicable rules. A strong sponsor can improve execution, but the property, transaction and requested terms also need to fit.

## Check the dates on the evidence

For the apartment purchase, ask for a current rent roll, a trailing 12-month operating statement, a year-to-date update if needed, and an explanation of recent changes in occupancy and collections. Request enough detail to locate concessions, bad debt and any one-time expense. Determine whether taxes, insurance or management costs could change after the sale. A future property tour, appraisal, environmental review or condition assessment may resolve risks this first pass can only flag.

If a seller supplied the T-12 before granting new concessions, the current rent roll and recent collections may give a different picture. Record the date and source of each input. Separate verified facts, borrower estimates and analyst assumptions in the screening record so the next reviewer can see what still needs support.

## Make the preliminary sizing assumptions visible

Assume the T-12 reports $650,000 in NOI, the analyst is testing $590,000 after tentative adjustments for recurring costs, and a $6.0 million loan is estimated to require $480,000 in annual principal and interest. These are fictional figures; the $480,000 is a supplied payment assumption, not a rate quote. Under a simplified NOI-based convention, indicated debt service coverage is about **1.23×** on the adjusted basis ($590,000 ÷ $480,000), versus about **1.35×** on the reported basis. If annual debt service were $510,000, adjusted coverage would be about **1.16×**. Compare compatible periods and verify the lender's cash-flow and reserve convention before using any threshold.

The requested loan is **66.7% of the contract price**. If the supported value instead came in at $8.4 million, the same loan would imply **71.4% loan-to-value**. The price ratio is an early reference point. The applicable value and permitted leverage require their own evidence. No number here is an eligibility rule, offer or commitment.

## Record what would move the request forward

- **Fit.** Confirm the lender considers this asset type, location, borrower and loan purpose. Stabilized, transitional and construction requests call for different evidence. An advertised multifamily program does not by itself establish eligibility.
- **Risk to investigate.** In this example, confirm the expense adjustments, whether concessions are still increasing, available equity, and whether the timing allows the necessary reports and decision.
- **Decision.** Advance to diligence if the request remains plausible with supportable assumptions. Ask for the missing information or a smaller request if the current numbers depend on a favorable assumption. Route to an appropriate alternative, or stop the screen, if there is no credible path.

Write down who owns the next action. The next analyst should know whether to reconcile income, verify sponsor capacity, ask for a revised capital plan or wait for a closing-date extension. The published T-12, rent roll, revenue underwriting and DSCR guides show how the early questions become documented financial and credit analysis.
