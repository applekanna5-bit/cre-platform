“We need a $6 million loan” tells you how much the borrower wants. It leaves open what the money will do, whether any existing debt survives closing and how the new debt will be repaid. Those answers tell you more about the transaction than the label on a term sheet.

## Buying a property or replacing its debt

**Acquisition financing** funds part of a purchase. A reader should be able to reconcile the contract price, transaction costs, any initial reserves and the equity contribution. The contract price is evidence of what the parties agreed to pay. It may differ from the lender's supported value. Review the income and liabilities the buyer will inherit and determine what changes at closing.

In a **rate-and-term refinance**, new debt replaces existing debt, primarily to change terms such as rate, maturity or amortization. A **cash-out refinance** also replaces the existing loan but produces net proceeds beyond the payoff and transaction uses, which can be released to the borrower or owners as permitted. Classification and permissible uses depend on the applicable program and documents. Start with a dated payoff that captures any relevant prepayment cost, then itemize fees, escrows and the destination of any remaining proceeds.

Fictional example. The existing balance is assumed to equal a $5.0 million payoff. Closing costs and required funding uses are $100,000, with no prepayment charge. A $5.1 million replacement pays those uses and leaves no net cash. A $6.0 million replacement would leave $900,000 before any additional uses or restrictions. The second request puts more debt against the same asset. The extra proceeds deserve their own use-of-funds and sizing discussion.

## Adding debt or financing a transition

**Supplemental financing** adds a loan while the existing loan remains outstanding. Consider a fictional $5 million first mortgage with a proposed $750,000 supplemental loan. Combined principal would be $5.75 million. Assess the two payment streams, lien priority, restrictions on additional debt and combined exposure. Some Fannie Mae multifamily executions advertise supplemental availability. The term sheet is a starting point for the product discussion; the governing guide and existing loan documents determine whether a particular request can proceed.

A **bridge loan** provides interim financing while the property or transaction moves toward a defined exit, such as completion of a renovation followed by sale or refinance. The case for a bridge loan rests on the work needed to get from current operations to the exit, the time and funds available, and how the borrower will repay if the schedule slips.

**Construction financing** supports project spending, may fund through draws, and requires a view of budget, completion and takeout risk. A **permanent loan** provides longer-term financing, often on completed income-producing property. Some structures combine construction and permanent phases, with conversion governed by stated conditions. A projected takeout is a repayment assumption until its terms and conditions are supported. In a fictional redevelopment scheduled to finish three months before maturity, model what happens if completion is six months late or the replacement lender sizes to lower income.

## When an existing loan stays in place

A **loan assumption** can allow a new borrower to take on the debt under the existing loan, subject to its documents and required consents. An **ownership transfer** may change interests or control in the entity that already owns the property, without changing the borrower. A **collateral transfer** can refer to conveyance of the encumbered property or a change in the assets securing a facility. Confirm what is moving and read the applicable provisions on transfers, releases, substitutions and consents. Approval of a transfer does not itself release the original borrower or guarantor from all obligations. A transfer or assumption may involve credit analysis and closing documents while leaving the existing loan in place.

Freddie Mac's public Guide chapter on transfers of ownership describes a servicer process for its covered transactions and distinguishes a property transfer with assumption from other changes. It illustrates a product-specific path. Applicable documents and approval authority still need to be verified for each loan.
