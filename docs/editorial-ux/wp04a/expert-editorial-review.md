## Scope limits and missing concepts

- **Cash flow conventions.** The published NOI and DSCR guides already explain Fannie Mae Underwritten NOI versus Underwritten NCF and the OCC convention. The indexed Fannie Mae §203.01 table corroborated the reserve placement; direct access to the live page returned HTTP 403 during the review. The drafts use the verified OCC convention and explicit fictional adjustments, without carrying a Fannie Mae NCF requirement into the example. A future claim about the operative Guide should be checked against the actual version, table and applicable product overlays. [Fannie Mae §203.01](https://mfguide.fanniemae.com/node/7531)
- **Financing channels.** “Conventional,” “bridge,” and “securitized” answer different questions. The DUS term sheet itself uses “Conventional Properties” within a Fannie Mae channel. The articles now make financing purpose independent of the funding channel. They leave product comparison, relative pricing, defeasance, intercreditor terms and securitization priorities beyond an introductory definition to later work. [Fannie Mae DUS term sheet](https://multifamily.fanniemae.com/financing-options/dus-mortgage-loans-term-sheet)
- **Authority and scope.** Broker and banker duties vary with the agreement and firm. Industry usage supports the distinctions, while licensing, rights and funding commitments require the actual facts and jurisdiction. No licensing claim is made. Affordable housing restrictions likewise depend on the applicable program and restrictions. [Mortgage Bankers Association on commercial correspondent arrangements](https://www.mba.org/docs/default-source/cmf-policy/cmf-white-papers-and-fact-sheets/cmf_pol_mba_paper_on_role_of_insurance_companies_in_cre-april2018.pdf)
- **Formal sizing.** The screen is a decision about further work. Debt yield, reserve funding, stressed payments and an independently supported value can affect formal sizing. A complete worked multi-constraint sizing exercise would belong in Stage 4. The screen leaves that work to the published DSCR guide and the planned sizing coverage.

## Writing and consistency

The cluster keeps five distinct forms: an asset comparison, a conversation about counterparties, a chronological handoff, a transaction comparison and a worked intake. The edits replace abstract introductions and process jargon where they got in the way of the decision. The reserve example is the only new numerical scenario, and it extends the original retail figures. The screen retains its stated dollar inputs and ratios. All examples remain explicitly fictional.

The eight-article comparison preserved these distinctions:

- **T-12 and Rent Roll Analysis.** Historical results cover a period; lease information is dated evidence. The screening revision tests events after the reporting period.
- **Contract Rent vs. Market Rent, GPI/EGI and CRE Revenue Underwriting.** Contractual rent, booked revenue and collections are separate. A rent gap or reimbursement cannot automatically be carried into a projection; the retail example pairs the reimbursement with its related cost.
- **NOI, Capitalization Rate and DSCR.** Reported and underwritten income can differ. Reserves are identified by convention, rates and income use the same basis, and coverage uses comparable periods. The drafts add no institutional minimum or approval test.

The approved seven stages and primary placements remain unchanged. Assumption and ownership-transfer requests can start in servicing and return to selected credit and closing steps. Loan sales and securitization remain linked to Financing & Markets. The existing ID relationships still lead from the five drafts to one another and all eight published guides. The [reading map](README.md#learning-and-link-map) lists these choices without publishing draft URLs.
