A buyer may first talk to a mortgage broker, close with a lender and later send a consent request to a servicer whose name never appeared in the original financing conversation. That can happen without the buyer taking out another loan. In U.S. commercial mortgage lending, arranging a loan, owning it and administering it are different jobs. One institution may do several of them.

## Who shapes the original credit decision?

The **borrower** is the legal party that owes the debt. The **sponsor** leads the investment and may arrange equity, oversee operations or sign a separate guarantee. The sponsor, borrower and guarantor can be different parties. A lender needs to know which party owns the collateral, which party controls decisions and who has committed to provide support.

A **mortgage broker** can assemble a financing request and place it with lenders. A **mortgage banker** may originate or arrange loans and, depending on its business model, close, sell or service them. The job title alone does not identify the funding source or credit authority. Ask in whose name the loan closes, who is committing funds and who can approve a policy exception.

The **originating lender** takes the loan through its underwriting and approval process. An **underwriter** tests the income, collateral, sponsor and proposed terms. A credit committee or other authorized approver decides whether to accept the risk and on what conditions. Decision authority varies by lender and transaction. An appraiser provides an opinion of value under a defined assignment. Environmental consultants, engineers, property-condition assessors, surveyors and counsel answer different questions, each within the scope of their assignment. A valuation conclusion does not decide whether the sponsor or loan structure is acceptable.

## Where the capital comes from

A bank, credit union or insurer can fund a loan for its own portfolio. A private debt fund may take a different view of a renovation or lease-up. A commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS) originator may expect a loan to be included in a securitization. These are channels with different funding models and credit policies. The right comparison is the purpose, collateral, term, flexibility and exit of the actual offer.

In multifamily lending, **Fannie Mae** and **Freddie Mac** are government-sponsored enterprises operating in the secondary market. Fannie Mae works with Delegated Underwriting and Servicing lenders. Freddie Mac purchases multifamily loans from its Optigo lender network. A borrower works with an originating lender. An enterprise's purchase or guarantee role depends on the execution. Federal Housing Administration multifamily mortgage insurance is a different form of government support. HUD describes insurance for eligible financing originated by FHA-approved lenders. That program structure is distinct from an enterprise mortgage purchase.

## A loan can change hands while the borrower stays in place

In a **whole-loan sale**, an investor acquires the loan itself. In a **securitization**, a trust can hold loans and issue securities whose payments depend on a defined allocation of cash flows and losses. A **tranche** is a class of securities with a specified place in that allocation. A certificate investor generally holds a security issued by the trust. The loan sale or security issuance sits in the financing and markets track. It is separate from the borrower's original request and can occur without a new borrowing.

Suppose a fictional lender closes an $8 million apartment mortgage, sells it into a pool and retains a borrower-facing servicing assignment. The owner later asks to release money held for roof work. The owner may contact the same company, even though the company no longer owns the loan. The controlling documents determine whether the company can approve the release or must send it elsewhere.

## Which servicer handles the request?

- A **primary servicer** may be the borrower's day-to-day contact for payment administration, financial reporting and requests.
- A **subservicer** performs tasks under a delegation. The delegated work can include borrower contact without transferring the appointing servicer's responsibilities under the governing agreement.
- A **master servicer** in a securitization typically administers performing loans at the pool level and coordinates duties such as remittances and oversight of delegated work.
- A **special servicer** typically takes responsibility for specified loans when transfer triggers are met and evaluates resolution under the applicable servicing standard and consent provisions. A transfer trigger can be more specific than a missed payment.
