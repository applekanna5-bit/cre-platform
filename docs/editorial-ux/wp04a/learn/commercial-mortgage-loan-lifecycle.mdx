A request comes in for a loan on a building the borrower wants to buy. The first conversation is about the amount and the closing date. Months after funding, the question may be whether a tenant departure affects a reserve release. These questions belong to the same loan, but they call for different evidence and different authority.

The seven stages below are a reading path for U.S. commercial mortgage finance. They follow a typical new loan. The sequence can overlap or repeat. A lender may order diligence while underwriting is in progress; a material change in rent or sponsorship may send an approved file back for review.

## 1. CRE Fundamentals and Property Types

Understand the property before choosing a product. Identify its ownership, income model and operating risks. A lease schedule helps with an apartment or office property. A hotel analyst needs operating records that explain room demand and expenses. At this stage the question is what economic activity could support repayment.

## 2. Loan Request and Preliminary Screening

Record the loan purpose, amount, proposed borrower and sponsor, and the required date. Reconcile the first sources and uses and ask where the equity or payoff money comes from. Screen the property and request against potential lenders, then decide whether more work is justified. Indicative terms or interest in the transaction are provisional.

## 3. Rent Roll, T-12 and Financial Analysis

A rent roll is a dated schedule of units or leased space and their terms. A trailing 12-month operating statement, or **T-12**, reports a period of financial performance. Compare them against one another and investigate gaps. A current rent roll may include a lease signed after the T-12 ended. A concession shown in a lease may have been booked in a different month. Timing is often the first explanation to test.

## 4. Underwriting, NOI and Loan Sizing

Translate operating evidence into an estimate of sustainable income under a stated convention. Test debt service coverage, leverage and sensitivity to weaker cash flow or different loan terms. A value-supported loan amount can still be too large for the income, or the reverse. Record the constraint and the assumptions behind it so a reviewer can tell what would change the result.

## 5. Credit Review, Third-Party Reports and Approval

Reconcile the underwriting with appraisal, environmental and property-condition findings where applicable. Review borrower and sponsor capacity, collateral and exceptions. Credit approval should identify its conditions and decision authority. An appraisal can support a collateral conclusion while a repair or an unresolved consent still affects whether the loan can close.

## 6. Closing and Funding

Confirm that required conditions have been satisfied or dispositioned by the appropriate authority. Counsel and closing staff deal with loan documents, security instruments, title, insurance, escrow and funding mechanics as applicable. Transfer operating instructions to servicing, including payment dates, reserve requirements and reporting obligations. An approval is an authorization subject to its terms. Funding is a separate event.

## 7. Loan Servicing, Asset Management and Special Servicing

Servicing starts by boarding the loan, meaning loading its terms and instructions into the servicing system and reconciling them with the closing record. Routine work can include payments, reserves, statements and borrower requests. Lender-side asset management monitors performance, covenant compliance and emerging risk. Where special servicing exists, transfer depends on the governing agreement and its triggers. Resolution might be ordinary payoff at sale or refinance, or, on a distressed loan, a negotiated accommodation, workout or enforcement path. Authority and options depend on the documents and applicable law.

## One closing condition over the life of a loan

In an expressly fictional acquisition, a property-condition report identifies a $180,000 roof replacement. The lender approves the loan subject to a repair escrow funded from borrower equity. The closing team verifies the deposit and records the release conditions. The servicer later receives the invoice and inspection evidence and checks whether the reserve may be released under the agreement. The repair estimate mattered at credit review, the cash mattered at closing, and the evidence and delegated authority matter at release.

A refinance starts with an existing loan and needs a payoff and replacement financing plan. A buyer seeking an assumption starts with the existing loan and its consent process, then may revisit selected credit and closing steps. A sale of the funded loan or a securitization belongs to the connected financing and markets track. Neither requires an eighth stage.
