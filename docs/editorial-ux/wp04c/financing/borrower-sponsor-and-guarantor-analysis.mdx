A borrower can own the real estate and have little cash outside it. The people who control the borrower may have considerable wealth, yet only one entity may have agreed to support the loan. Draw the ownership and control structure first. Then identify whose money is available, when it can be used and what the lender can enforce.

## Trace control and support separately

The borrower signs the note and owns the collateral interest in a typical property-level loan. The sponsor organizes the investment, contributes or raises equity and directs the business plan. A guarantor signs a separate undertaking and may be a sponsor, affiliate or another party. One person or entity can serve several roles. Trace control through managers, general partners and approval rights. A large passive investor may lack authority to direct a capital call, while a manager with a small equity interest can control operations.

For a property-owning entity, reconcile cash and liabilities to the property underwriting and closing sources. Check affiliate payables, subordinate debt, distributions and whether the acquisition equity has actually been committed and can be called before settlement. For sponsorship, examine operating experience with comparable assets, lease-up or construction capability where relevant, performance under pressure and who handles property management. Read any required independent management or succession arrangements alongside the ownership chart.

## Quantify available support over the period that matters

Build from dated financial statements, schedules of real estate and debt, cash and brokerage evidence, and disclosures of contingent liabilities. Tie significant balances back to the party that owns them. An account in an affiliate or jointly owned vehicle may require consent to access. Assets can be pledged, restricted or subject to tax and sale costs. Compare loan maturities, other guarantees and expected equity calls over the same window. Use current evidence when a material balance or obligation has changed since the statement date.

In the fictional shopping-center acquisition, the sponsor reports $2.4 million in cash and marketable securities. Assume $500,000 is pledged and $700,000 is committed to another project over the next six months. That leaves $1.2 million before any further obligations. If the same pool must also provide $900,000 of the equity wired at this closing, only $300,000 remains on those assumptions. That $900,000 is part of total closing equity, including the sponsor's contribution toward settlement uses. It is not incremental funding on top of the settlement statement. A separate property-level deficit and a guaranty call could compete for the $300,000. The amount requires verification and represents a point-in-time estimate of accessible support.

Construction and lease-up experience can help assess willingness and execution. The lender still needs to understand the support it can legally call for, which entity holds the assets and whether a capital call is enforceable and timely. Avoid double-counting the same liquid funds across related guarantors or assuming a minority share of a property's equity can be sold on demand.

## Read each undertaking for its trigger and measure of liability

With full payment recourse, a liable party may be responsible for the debt as specified in the documents and subject to applicable law. Limited support may cover a fixed amount, a share of principal, carry costs or completion. A burn-off can reduce or release an obligation after documented conditions are met. Check who confirms satisfaction, whether notice is needed and which other obligations survive.

In a nonrecourse structure, the lender's ordinary recovery is generally limited to the collateral and agreed remedies. Carve-outs can impose liability for specific losses, such as losses caused by misappropriated rents. Other specified conduct or events can trigger liability for the entire debt where the agreement provides for it. A separate environmental indemnity can operate on its own terms, including survival after repayment if the documents so provide. Read the definitions, triggers, caps, exceptions and remedy language with counsel. A guarantee label or term-sheet description cannot settle whether a particular event triggers loss-only or full-debt liability.

## Put the support into the approval and the servicing record

Credit needs the named obligor, capacity, proposed obligations and conditions of release. Closing needs authority to execute, signatures and consistent entity names throughout the documents. Servicing needs the signed undertaking and the reporting dates or tests that apply after funding. A future transfer of the property, sponsor interest or loan must be reviewed under the relevant consent and release terms.

Portfolio lenders may negotiate the scope of support within their policies. Fannie Mae Multifamily loans use applicable program forms and approved modifications. For a loan intended for a private-label transaction, review the purchaser's requirements and the actual negotiated recourse provisions. The loan channel can indicate where to look for the governing rules. The signed agreement defines the undertaking in the individual file.
