A report earns its place in the file by answering a question about the collateral. An appraisal asks what interest is worth. An environmental assessment looks for signs of contamination risk. A survey locates physical features and certain recorded matters. The lender and counsel have to read the answers together before they can decide whether the property supports the proposed loan.

## Set scope and reliance early

Use the correct address, parcels, legal interest, intended users and transaction purpose when ordering reports. Confirm the lender's engagement, independence and reliance requirements. An appraisal prepared for a seller or an environmental report addressed to a prior lender may offer leads. Its use in the new credit file depends on the relevant rules, report terms and any acceptable reliance or update. Keep dates visible. An inspection date, report date and valuation effective date can differ, and a new lease or casualty may change the relevant facts before closing.

For valuation, check the interest appraised, the value date and whether the conclusion is as-is, as-complete or stabilized. A leased-fee appraisal and the underwriter's cash-flow model can apply different lease or reserve assumptions. Reconcile major differences in rents, vacancy, expenses and capitalization to the evidence rather than averaging the two results. Review material assumptions, comparable sales or leases and any conditions attached to the conclusion. In regulated bank lending, applicable appraisal or evaluation rules and independent review requirements matter alongside the lender's policy.

## Follow environmental and engineering findings to a decision

A Phase I environmental site assessment reviews historical uses, records and site observations within its scope. It generally does not establish contamination concentrations through sampling. A former dry cleaner beside the fictional shopping center may prompt an environmental professional to recommend targeted investigation. Before scheduling a closing, confirm access to investigate, understand what the results could mean for collateral and identify who will pay for any further work. Testing, remediation, monitoring, controls on future use or a decision to pause may follow. The environmental professional and counsel address different questions about the same finding.

EPA recognizes ASTM E1527-21 for satisfying the federal All Appropriate Inquiries rule, with the recognition effective February 13, 2023. For acquisitions relying on that rule, inquiries are conducted or updated within one year before acquisition, with certain components conducted or updated within 180 days. Those clocks relate to acquisition and potential CERCLA liability protections. The lender's report currency and reliance rules may impose separate requirements, and All Appropriate Inquiries is one element of qualifying for relevant protections.

A property condition assessment looks at the physical systems, observed deficiencies and expected capital needs, subject to its inspection limits. Check whether the engineer visited the areas driving the conclusion and whether the cost estimate includes the complete scope, associated work and timing. At the shopping center, a $120,000 roof estimate matters to the repair escrow. A release condition can call for defined work, satisfactory inspection and evidence that contractor claims have been addressed under the documents. Compare short-term repair needs with recurring replacements in the cash-flow analysis so a cost appears on the right schedule.

## Read insurance, title and survey side by side with the leases

Insurance review compares actual coverage evidence against the loan requirements. Confirm insured parties, lender endorsements or designations, limits, deductibles, effective dates and material exclusions. Consider property and liability coverage, business income and relevant location-specific hazards. A certificate may summarize coverage. The policy and endorsements establish what it provides. An insurance cost or deductible that changes during diligence can require a cash-flow or liquidity review.

Title review checks vesting, the land description, proposed lien priority and exceptions such as easements, restrictions or existing liens. A survey provides evidence about boundaries, improvements, encroachments and access. Counsel should compare the two and trace material exceptions to the underlying instruments. If the survey shows the only truck route crosses a neighboring parcel, confirm whether recorded rights actually cover that route and use. If a key lease depends on that access, review its remedies and the impact on cash flow. Possible responses include obtaining rights, addressing coverage with the title insurer or revisiting approval. The parties need to know which response secures the property interest the lender approved.

Also check relevant use and zoning evidence, leases and estoppels, priority and nondisturbance arrangements, and the authority of the borrower and other obligors. A lender financing a leasehold would also examine the ground lease, remaining term and lender protections. Counsel evaluates the governing law and the legal effect of the documents. Credit evaluates the remaining exposure.

## Put dated requirements in the checklist

A portfolio lender may prescribe its own report scope and reviewers within the applicable regulatory framework. For Fannie Mae Multifamily loans, Guide Update 26-06 applies to surveys contracted for on or after March 11, 2026. It aligns the guide with the 2026 ALTA survey standards and adds a Table A encroachment-table requirement. Verify the applicable edition, options and transaction-specific guide provisions with the program lender. For a planned private-label execution, confirm intended purchaser standards for acceptable reports and reliance before ordering.
