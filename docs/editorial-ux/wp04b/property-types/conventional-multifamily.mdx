An apartment property can report 95% occupancy while its rental collections weaken. A unit occupied under a lease with a free month looks full on the rent roll. It produces less income than the advertised rent suggests. Start with the leases and the ledger together, then ask which results can carry into the loan term.

## Read the unit mix before the average rent

Garden apartments, mid-rise buildings and high-rises can all serve the same rental market, but their operating costs differ. Elevators, structured parking and staffed common areas change the expense and capital budget. Descriptions such as Class A or Class B are market shorthand. Ask what the label means in that market and which properties prospective tenants actually compare. A stabilized property and one undergoing renovation can also look alike from the street. In the second case, test the cost and downtime of taking units out of service before assuming a rent premium.

Group the rent roll by bedroom count, size and meaningful differences in condition or lease terms. For each group, identify occupied units, vacant units available to rent, units down for work, model and employee units, leases signed but not yet started, and upcoming expirations. Keep a consistent inventory denominator. A unit taken offline for repairs still matters to the investment even when an operating report excludes it from its advertised occupancy.

**Contract rent** is the rent in the signed lease. **Market rent** is an estimate for comparable space on comparable terms. Compare recent new and renewal leases with like units, then adjust the comparison for incentives. A one-bedroom advertised at $1,500 with one month free on a twelve-month term has a simple first-term effective rent of $1,375 per month, before any other charges or collection loss. An apparent rent increase at renewal may shrink once the incentive is included. In-place leases also determine when any increase can occur.

## Occupancy tells three different stories

Physical occupancy counts occupied units against the defined inventory. Economic occupancy needs a stated revenue numerator and denominator. A collections-based measure can differ from one based on recognized rent after concessions and credit losses. Stabilized occupancy is the level assumed to be sustainable after temporary conditions such as lease-up have passed. Support it with lease activity, turnover, local supply and comparable properties. A day-one occupancy figure or a recent leasing burst gives only part of that evidence.

Consider a fictional 100-unit property. Its annual scheduled rent basis is $1,872,000, using in-place rents for occupied units and assumed rents for vacant units. There are 95 occupied units on the observation date. For the forward year, assume $84,000 of vacancy loss, $24,000 of concessions and $12,000 of collection loss, with each deduction defined separately. The indicated net rental income is $1,752,000, or approximately 93.6% of the stated schedule. That is a modeled revenue realization ratio, not a record of cash received. The five vacant units on the rent-roll date do not by themselves determine the full-year dollar loss.

Add $60,000 in assumed recurring other income to arrive at $1,812,000 of effective gross income. Trace parking, laundry, storage and utility charges to leases and collections before treating them as durable. Separate refundable deposits and one-time items. If the ledger already nets concessions from rental income, reconcile that baseline before deducting any additional allowance.

## Underwrite what it costs to keep the units rentable

Read the trailing 12-month statement by month. A rising insurance premium may be visible only in the most recent months. A large repair can represent ordinary work concentrated in one month, an unusual event or deferred work finally completed. The invoice, contract and condition report answer different parts of that question. A buyer should investigate post-sale taxes and get insurance evidence for the projected ownership period. Historical accruals on their own do not establish either cost.

Look for utilities paid by the owner, usage changes, utility reimbursements and shortfalls in recovery. Pair reimbursements with the underlying costs. If management expense is low because the seller self-manages, consider what ongoing management would cost. Keep regular maintenance in operating expenses. Track replacements such as roofs, HVAC and major plumbing in the capital plan, with a clearly stated reserve convention.

Assume the same fictional property reported $660,000 of annual operating expenses. The analyst adds $30,000 for insurance and $18,000 for property taxes, and removes $8,000 for a documented one-time operating repair while retaining ordinary maintenance. The annual expense estimate is $700,000. Against the stated revenue, that gives $1,112,000 of **pre-reserve net operating income (NOI)**. The figures illustrate a line-by-line adjustment. The actual adjustment in a loan file needs its own evidence.

## Carry the same assumptions into value and credit

At a hypothetical 5.75% capitalization rate on a matching pre-reserve basis, the example indicates about $19.34 million. That rate is a teaching assumption. A property in lease-up or facing major near-term work may need explicit forecasts for those costs and timing. For a separate credit illustration, deduct a $45,000 annual replacement allowance to give $1,067,000 of defined cash flow. With $800,000 in assumed annual principal and interest, coverage is about 1.33×. The value and coverage answer different questions. Loan sizing also depends on leverage, payment assumptions, sponsor support and any other applicable tests.

“Conventional multifamily” can describe the asset or an agency product. It does not identify who keeps the loan. Fannie Mae's Delegated Underwriting and Servicing (DUS) term sheet includes conventional properties in its permanent multifamily offering. Freddie Mac purchases loans through Optigo lenders. A bank or insurer may make a portfolio loan under its own policy. Compare the actual cash-flow definitions and reserve treatment, reporting and transfer provisions, prepayment terms and maturity, rather than assuming one set of terms across channels.
