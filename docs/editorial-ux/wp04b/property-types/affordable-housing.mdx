Two apartments with identical floor plans can have different legal rent limits, different payers and different rules for the next lease. In affordable housing, the unit roster and the recorded agreements tell you which income can be earned. A market survey shows what comparable housing might command. It cannot override a rent restriction or create a subsidy payment.

## Build a unit-level list of restrictions and payers

An income restriction governs which households may qualify under a program or agreement. A rent restriction governs how much can be charged under a specified definition. Either can apply to only part of a property. An apartment can also receive rental assistance, which determines who pays part of the approved rent. Trace these items separately. Lower market rents alone do not tell you whether an enforceable restriction exists.

For a purchase or refinance, gather the recorded use agreements, credit allocation and election records where applicable, assistance contracts, rent and utility-allowance schedules, the tenant certification roster and any monitoring findings. Record which units or groups are covered, relevant dates and the organizations that enforce the terms. Where restrictions overlap, determine how each applies to the same unit. The applicable limits and permissible charges may require specialist review before any rent change is underwritten.

## LIHTC funds development equity and brings ongoing obligations

The federal **Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC)** under Internal Revenue Code §42 supports qualifying rental housing. Housing credit agencies administer allocations or applicable determinations. An owner can bring in an investor through a partnership or similar structure, with the investor contributing equity in exchange for an interest that can receive tax benefits. The tax credits and equity installments belong in the development financing analysis. They are separate from rent a property earns in ordinary operations.

Section 42 generally distinguishes the ten-taxable-year credit period, the fifteen-taxable-year compliance period and the extended use commitment. Under the federal definition, extended use ordinarily runs at least fifteen additional years after the compliance period, subject to statutory provisions. The recorded agreement or other financing can impose longer restrictions. Review the placed-in-service and election records, plus the actual agreements and amendments. Year 15 does not by itself establish a right to raise rents, release a restriction or sell without consent.

LIHTC gross rent limits use an imputed income limit for the unit. Confirm the applicable income band and the unit's treatment under the project's election before selecting a rent schedule. The ceiling is calculated under program rules and is distinct from the tenant's verified household income. The statutory definition generally includes an applicable utility allowance and excludes qualifying Section 8 or comparable rental assistance payments. That definition is different from the rent charged to an individual household or the amount received by the owner. Tenant income certification supports eligibility, and any recertification obligations and exceptions depend on the applicable program, project and monitoring rules. Maintain the supporting records and track agency findings, corrective actions and reporting. A unit can be occupied and paying rent while the owner's file is missing evidence needed to demonstrate compliance.

## Reconcile the rent cap with money received

A **Housing Assistance Payments (HAP)** contract governs assistance for covered units. HUD describes project-based vouchers (PBV) as tied to specified units through a public housing agency and describes Project-Based Rental Assistance (PBRA) as a separate HUD multifamily program. Tenant-based vouchers generally follow the eligible household rather than being assigned to a specific unit by a project-based contract. In either case, confirm how the owner's payments are authorized and recorded. Verify the actual contract, which units it covers, the tenant and subsidy portions, payment history, renewal or expiration, and conditions such as inspections that can affect payments. Separate the risk of late subsidy payments from recurring tenant collection loss.

<SourceNote sourceId="wp04b-hud-pbv" />

Consider a fictional 60-unit property. Forty LIHTC units do not receive rental assistance. For this example, assume each has a $1,150 monthly gross rent ceiling, a $90 tenant-paid utility allowance and no additional charges counted in gross rent. That leaves up to $1,060 as the lease rent under this constraint. The actual lease rent is $1,000. Use $1,000 until the file supports an increase, including compliance with other applicable limits. The ceiling and allowance are fictional inputs, not published limits.

The other 20 units are assumed compliant with applicable restrictions and covered by an assistance contract. The illustrative approved owner rent is $1,200 per month, made up of $350 from the tenant and $850 in assistance. The $1,200 is total scheduled owner rent; adding $850 again would overstate income. The example supplies no calculation of a LIHTC cap for those assisted units.

At full scheduled billing, annual rent is $768,000. Assume $30,000 in distinct vacancy and credit losses and $12,000 of recurring permitted other income. Fees and reimbursements need their own compliance and collectibility review. The result is $750,000 in effective gross income. Compare that amount with recent cash receipts by payer. If the assistance contract expires during the proposed loan, analyze the downside and any evidence for renewal. Do not replace a lost payment with unrestricted market rent unless the actual agreements support that change.

## Size the loan around the property and the restrictions

Management and compliance staffing, insurance, utilities and ongoing repairs require realistic budgets. Tax exemptions or abatements can depend on ownership and continuing eligibility, so verify the terms and timing of any benefit. Keep capital needs and required reserve deposits visible, especially after rehabilitation. An appraisal should explain which restrictions and assistance contracts the value assumes, their remaining terms and what transfers with a sale. A rent-restricted asset with expiring assistance cannot be valued simply by capitalizing unrestricted market rents.

Suppose expenses are $430,000 a year. Pre-reserve NOI is $320,000. Under a stated credit convention that sets aside $30,000 for replacement reserves, defined cash flow before debt service is $290,000. Annual senior debt service of $220,000 gives about 1.32× coverage on the senior debt alone. If a subordinate loan also requires $30,000 in annual cash payments, coverage on those combined assumed obligations is 1.16×. A deferred or surplus-cash note would require a different analysis. Confirm whether payment can accrue, when it is due, what proceeds can be distributed, and when lender or agency consent is needed. Even a subordinate note with no scheduled cash payments can affect refinancing through its maturity and lien terms.

The financing may combine investor equity, a senior mortgage, public subordinate loans, grants or other funds. Track the conditions for each funding source at closing, through construction or rehabilitation, and during operations. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac offer dedicated affordable multifamily financing channels. FHA insurance, local funding and portfolio lending are other possible routes depending on the project. A product overview establishes that a channel exists. The operative guide and the actual restrictions, subsidies and documents determine eligibility and structure.
