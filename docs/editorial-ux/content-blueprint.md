# Content blueprint

The CRE Ledger should give a reader enough context to follow a commercial real estate loan and enough detail to understand the decision at each handoff. The initial editorial scope is U.S. income-producing CRE. Name the jurisdiction, transaction documents, product and date whenever they change the analysis. This specification is based on the reviewed production source at `05880f6`. The separate M3.35 draft is a design reference only.

## Lifecycle and editorial ownership

Each article has one primary stage for placement and review. The other stages can link to it where the same analysis matters. The owners below are review responsibilities to assign before commissioning, with a named reviewer recorded only when a real person has performed the review.

| Stage and proposed anchor | Subject taxonomy | Decision or handoff | Editorial review lead |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. CRE Fundamentals and Property Types · `fundamentals` | Property income and costs, lease structures, tenants, owners and lenders, collateral and sponsor, property categories and operating models, purchase versus lending decisions | Recognize the asset, income model, parties and purpose of the loan | Foundations and property editor |
| 2. Loan Request and Preliminary Screening · `origination` | Borrower request, uses and sources, lender and product fit, sponsor and market screen, early documents, indicative terms, initial risk questions, screening outcomes | Decide what merits further diligence and what evidence is missing | Origination and financing editor |
| 3. Rent Roll, T-12 and Financial Analysis · `financial-analysis` | Lease-level data, trailing 12-month statements, occupancy and collections, contract versus market rent, concessions, GPR/GPI/EGI, revenue support, expenses, capital needs and reserve inputs | Reconcile dated operating evidence and identify a supportable revenue and expense basis | Property financial analysis editor |
| 4. Underwriting, NOI and Loan Sizing · `loan-sizing` | Reported versus underwritten NOI and NCF conventions, stabilization, cap rates and valuation, DSCR/LTV/debt yield, term versus amortization, sizing constraints and sensitivities | Explain a credit sizing conclusion and which assumptions could change it | Underwriting editor with independent calculation review |
| 5. Credit Review, Third-Party Reports and Approval · `credit-review` | Sponsor and guarantor evaluation, appraisal, environmental and property-condition diligence, report scope and exceptions, credit memo, independent review, approval conditions | Connect risks and mitigants to a documented decision and open conditions | Credit editor with report specialists as needed |
| 6. Closing and Funding · `closing` | Commitment versus conditions, title and survey, insurance, organizational diligence, loan documents, escrows, funding and boarding handoff | Establish that closing conditions are satisfied and identify duties that transfer to servicing | Closing editor with legal review for rights or obligations |
| 7. Loan Servicing, Asset Management and Special Servicing · `servicing` | Boarding, payments and reporting, escrow and reserves, covenants, consent requests, inspections, watchlists, collateral monitoring, transfers, default, workouts and resolution | Determine who has authority, what needs monitoring or escalation, and which paths are available | Servicing and asset management editor with workout/legal review as applicable |

Stages describe the typical questions in an origination-led learning sequence. A refinance brings an existing balance, payoff and maturity into screening. A bridge loan starts with a transition and exit plan, with underwriting adjusted to that risk. An assumption or transfer generally starts with a servicing or consent request on an existing loan and can require selected credit and closing steps. Cancellations, declines, waivers and repeated diligence belong in applicable examples. Every general flow should say where product terms or transaction documents can change it.

## Connected tracks

| Track | Topic ownership and scope | Learning connections |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agency lending | Financing & Markets owns the Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and agency lending comparison. Each institution has its own scope and sources. Underwriting owns shared cash-flow and sizing concepts. | Stage 2 lender fit → stages 3–5 agency-specific inputs and review, if publicly verifiable → stages 6–7 delivery and servicing. Explain the intended meaning of agency in an article’s opening scope. |
| Residential asset types | Property Types owns conventional multifamily, manufactured housing communities (MHC), affordable housing, senior housing and student housing. Use operating and income distinctions as the first comparison. Cross-link program tracks only where the property and product fit. | Stage 1 asset economics → stage 3 lease, occupancy and income evidence → stages 4 and 7 risk, sizing and monitoring. Separate affordability restrictions from building type, and define the senior housing care or service model. |
| Commercial and other property types | Property Types owns retail, office, industrial, hospitality and other asset types supported by evidence. Each guide defines its revenue model and major lease or operating risks. Mixed-use assets get an explicit component-level scope. | Stage 1 asset model → stages 2–4 lender fit, lease or operating evidence, rollover and sizing → stage 7 monitoring. Applicability of a multifamily example should be explicit. |
| Loan purposes and transactions | Financing & Markets owns acquisition, refinance, supplemental and bridge comparisons. Servicing owns post-closing assumptions and transfers, with financing and credit reviewers where underwriting is required. Distinguish a sale of the property or ownership interest from a sale of the mortgage loan. | Stage 2 purpose and inputs → stages 4–6 constraints and documentation → stage 7 consents, monitoring and repayment or exit. |
| Secondary markets and structured finance | Financing & Markets owns loan sales, securitization, CMBS pooling, tranches, cash-flow priorities, rating agencies and investor reporting. Compare whole-loan transfers and securitization as separate transactions where applicable. | Begin with the origination and funding handoff, then follow loan sale or pooling and security issuance; link to stage 7 for servicing rights and transaction-specific oversight. This is a connected track alongside the seven-stage loan lifecycle. |
| Servicing roles | Servicing owns primary servicing, subservicing, master servicing and special servicing. Describe responsibilities, delegation, transfer triggers and reporting with an identified agreement or program. | Stage 6 boarding handoff → stage 7 routine administration, delegation and escalation → CMBS or agency track for document-dependent authority. |
| Asset and default management | Servicing owns lender- and servicer-side asset management, default management, workouts and loan resolution. Distinguish these from the owner-side asset manager’s operating role and from securities administration. | Stage 7 monitoring and early warning → default evaluation and workout options → resolution; link to the applicable credit information and controlling documents. |

Track leads review cross-links and applicability. A topic gets one owning brief and one canonical destination. A rollup list can point to the same article from the lifecycle, a track and the Library. Add a comparison or deep dive when it answers a different question with evidence. Use smaller entries, examples or section anchors where they are sufficient.

| 4 | [Capitalization Rate and Property Valuation](/content/underwriting/capitalization-rate-property-valuation) · CRE-018. Use consistent income and rate assumptions in direct capitalization. |
| 4 | [Debt Service Coverage Ratio](/content/underwriting/debt-service-coverage-ratio-cre-underwriting) · CRE-025. Match defined cash flow and debt service periods and distinguish coverage from approval. |

Offer the published GPI/EGI guide as the first read for a newcomer. For a reader holding an operating statement or rent roll, start with that evidence. Then connect the revenue guide to NOI and offer valuation or coverage as the appropriate next decision. The numbers in Harbor View, Cedar Court and the separate homepage example must keep their own stated assumptions and identities. A later editor can change the suggested reading order without changing any of the articles. Stages 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 have planned coverage until they have published reading of their own.

## Contract and review boundaries

The reviewed source has coarse `ArticleDomain` values for delivery and separate `propertyTypes`, `loanProducts`, `topics` and typed relationships in [the content contracts](../../src/content/contracts.ts). The approved navigation labels and the lifecycle placements are discovery taxonomy. In a later implementation, prefer a typed presentation registry keyed by stable article IDs, with one stage assignment and zero or more track assignments. Validate references against the publication projection; keep MDX, sidecars, related-content purposes and canonical delivery paths intact. Any new delivery domain or contract change needs a separate architecture decision. [Content architecture](../content-architecture.md) · [ADR 0005](../adr/0005-local-mdx-content-engine.md)
