Dividing annual income by a rate can produce a precise-looking number from assumptions that are still uncertain. The calculation is useful when income and rate describe the same economic basis. It becomes misleading when the numerator changes convention while the denominator comes from an unrelated transaction.

The published NOI guide shows why a supported expense adjustment affects an illustrative value. This is a foundational explanation of direct capitalization, stated stabilized NOI, cap-rate mathematics and one illustrative property value. It does not select a market cap rate or provide an appraisal. Selecting a market-derived rate from comparable transactions requires separate evidence and analysis. Comparable transactions, cap-rate selection methodology, valuation sensitivity and advanced valuation judgment are reserved for a separate advanced article; this fundamentals article does not perform that work or replace an integrated valuation.

## What a capitalization rate expresses

A **capitalization rate** relates stated annual NOI to property value or transaction price on a consistent basis. For calculation, 5.50% means 0.055, not 5.5.

This illustration uses a positive capitalization rate. Division by a zero rate is undefined, and a negative or nonrepresentative NOI should not be turned into a market-value conclusion by mechanically applying this shortcut.

The OCC handbook describes direct capitalization for stabilized income expected to remain stable, and distinguishes it from discounted cash-flow analysis. That supports the general method, not Cedar Court's assumed rate or value.

A cap rate is not a mortgage interest rate, equity cash-on-cash return or multi-year internal rate of return. Those measures use different cash flows and capital bases. A change in loan interest does not mechanically change this example's unlevered property NOI.

## Establish the fictional income basis

Use Cedar Court, the fictional 100-unit illustration in Gross Potential Income vs. Effective Gross Income in CRE Underwriting. Annual GPR is $1,800,000. Subtract $90,000 vacancy, $18,000 concessions and $9,000 credit loss, then add $60,000 of net other income. EGI is $1,743,000.

Assume recurring operating expenses of **$697,200/year**, including property taxes, insurance, management, utilities and routine repairs. This fictional 40% of EGI is not a recommended expense ratio or a substitute for line-by-line underwriting. It excludes debt service, depreciation, owner income taxes, capital expenditure and replacement-reserve deductions.

## Calculate the illustrative value

Assume solely for this illustration that Cedar Court is stabilized and that the stated annual NOI represents the income basis to be capitalized. The 5.50% rate is hypothetical and is assumed to match that stated pre-reserve basis; no market suitability has been established.

Assume a **5.50% cap rate solely for education**. No market dataset supports this assumption.

## Interpret the income convention

The income definition must match the rate basis. Cedar Court's hypothetical 5.50% rate is paired only with the stated pre-reserve NOI. If another framework deducts a replacement reserve within NOI or uses a below-NOI cash-flow measure, identify that difference before applying a capitalization rate. A change in labels does not establish a supported rate or a market-value conclusion.

<ConventionNote>The OCC handbook's NOI definition includes replacement reserves. Cedar Court deliberately states a pre-reserve convention to expose the comparability issue. Neither numerator may be combined with a market-derived rate until definitions are aligned. This is a consistency exercise, not a universal lender rule.</ConventionNote>

## What the ratio leaves out

Direct capitalization compresses an income expectation into one ratio. Lease-up, major renovation, changing rents or exceptional capital needs may require more explicit timing analysis. This article does not model those paths or establish as-is or prospective market value.

Common errors include entering 5.5 instead of 0.055, capitalizing monthly rather than annual income, using gross revenue instead of stated NOI, deducting mortgage payments from the unlevered numerator, and choosing a rate to reverse-engineer a desired value. Retain precision until presenting the result.
