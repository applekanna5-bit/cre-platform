“We need a $6 million loan” tells you how much the borrower wants. It leaves open what the money will do, whether any existing debt survives closing and how the new debt will be repaid. Those answers tell you more about the transaction than the label on a term sheet. A bank and a securitizing lender can both finance an acquisition. Loan purpose, funding channel and loan structure are separate: even a loan described as conventional may be held in portfolio or sold.

## Buying a property or replacing its debt

**Acquisition financing** funds part of a purchase. A reader should be able to reconcile the contract price, transaction costs, any initial reserves and the equity contribution. The contract price is evidence of what the parties agreed to pay. It may differ from the lender's supported value. Review the income and liabilities the buyer will inherit and determine what changes at closing.

In a **rate-and-term refinance**, new debt replaces existing debt, primarily to change terms such as rate, maturity or amortization. A **cash-out refinance** also replaces the existing loan but produces net proceeds beyond the payoff and transaction uses, which can be released to the borrower or owners as permitted. The cash left in a simple funds-flow calculation does not settle the program classification. Confirm how the applicable lender or program treats costs, reimbursements and distributions. Start with a dated payoff that captures any relevant prepayment cost, then itemize fees, escrows and the destination of any remaining proceeds.

In one fictional refinance, the existing balance is assumed to equal a $5.0 million payoff. Closing costs and required funding uses are $100,000, with no prepayment charge. A $5.1 million replacement pays those uses and leaves no net cash. A $6.0 million replacement would leave $900,000 before any additional uses or restrictions. The second request puts more debt against the same asset. The extra proceeds deserve their own use-of-funds and sizing discussion.

## Adding debt or financing a transition

**Supplemental financing** adds a loan while the existing loan remains outstanding. Suppose a fictional first mortgage has a $5 million unpaid balance and the borrower requests a $750,000 supplemental loan. The combined balance would be $5.75 million at funding, assuming the first balance is unchanged and there is no other debt on the property. The $5.75 million is the combined debt balance. To estimate coverage, compare the cash flow with the payments on both loans under the lender's method. For a conventional lender considering a junior loan, check whether the senior documents permit additional debt and what consent would be needed. Ask the lender which balances, payments and valuation basis it uses. Freddie Mac's supplemental checklist, for example, records combined coverage and leverage alongside the requirements in the senior loan documents.

Fannie Mae's Part III, Chapter 13, in the edition effective June 2, 2026, illustrates a program-specific approach. The lowest applicable sizing result sets the maximum supplemental amount. The chapter uses its own amortizing payment assumptions to calculate combined debt service. Those underwriting payments can differ from the borrower's actual payments. The debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) uses the combined debt service, while loan-to-value (LTV) takes account of the unpaid balances of the covered existing mortgages and the proposed supplemental principal.

In that edition, if the senior loan matures within five years after origination of a Tier 2 supplemental loan, Fannie Mae's New Loan Test also checks whether the combined balance would fit within the amount supportable by a hypothetical new fixed-rate, first-lien cash-out mortgage. The test measures refinance capacity under the program's assumptions. Check transaction eligibility and the governing loan documents separately. A request can fit the sizing tests and still be ineligible.

A **bridge loan** provides interim financing while the property or transaction moves toward a defined exit, such as completion of a renovation followed by sale or refinance. The case for a bridge loan rests on the work needed to get from current operations to the exit, the time and funds available, and how the borrower will repay if the schedule slips.

**Construction financing** supports project spending, may fund through draws, and requires a view of budget, completion and the risk that expected long-term replacement financing does not close. A **permanent loan** provides longer-term financing, often on completed income-producing property. Some structures combine construction and permanent phases, with conversion governed by stated conditions. That replacement loan, often called a **takeout**, may depend on completion, leasing or other conditions. Check those conditions even when a lender has already issued a commitment. In a fictional redevelopment scheduled to finish three months before maturity, model what happens if completion is six months late or the replacement lender sizes to lower income.

## When an existing loan stays in place

A **loan assumption** can allow a new borrower to take on the debt under the existing loan, subject to its documents and required consents. An **ownership transfer** may change interests or control in the entity that already owns the property, without changing the borrower. A **collateral transfer** can refer to conveyance of the encumbered property or a change in the assets securing a facility. Confirm what is moving and read the applicable provisions on transfers, releases, substitutions and consents. Check the assumption and release documents separately to see which obligations, if any, the original borrower or guarantor keeps. A transfer or assumption can require fresh credit analysis and closing documents while the existing loan remains outstanding. If the same transaction includes a supplemental loan, the supplemental is a separate new origination.

Freddie Mac's public Guide chapter on transfers of ownership describes a servicer process for its covered transactions and distinguishes a property transfer with assumption from other changes. It illustrates a product-specific path. Applicable documents and approval authority still need to be verified for each loan.
