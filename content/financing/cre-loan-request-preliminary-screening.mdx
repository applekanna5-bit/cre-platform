The buyer of a 60-unit apartment property asks for $6 million and a closing in 45 days. Before talking pricing, find out whether the equity is ready, what the property earns and whether the lender can finish reviewing the file in time.

## Put the request in one sentence

In this fictional request, the 45 days are the buyer's target. Its purchase contract calls for $9.0 million. Estimated costs and initial funding needs add $300,000. Total uses are $9.3 million, so a $6.0 million loan needs $3.3 million of equity. Establish whether that equity is committed, when it is available and whether any of it comes from another borrowing. A call with the buyer can resolve ambiguity about the intended use of proceeds before the analyst starts sizing.

Identify the proposed borrower, its controlling parties and the sponsor. Ask about similar properties operated, who manages this one, relevant financial capacity, liquidity after closing and whether any guarantee is contemplated. Check the lender's actual eligibility policy and the program's applicable rules. A strong sponsor can improve execution, but the property, transaction and requested terms also need to fit.

## Check the dates on the evidence

For the apartment purchase, ask for a current rent roll, a trailing 12-month operating statement, a year-to-date update if needed, and an explanation of recent changes in occupancy and collections. Request enough detail to locate concessions, bad debt and any one-time expense. Determine whether taxes, insurance or management costs could change after the sale. A future property tour, appraisal, environmental review or condition assessment may resolve risks this first pass can only flag.

If the seller granted concessions after the T-12 reporting period, the current rent roll and recent collections may give a different picture. Record the date and source of each input. Separate verified facts, borrower estimates and analyst assumptions in the screening record so the next reviewer can see what still needs support.

## Make the preliminary sizing assumptions visible

Assume the seller's T-12 reports $650,000 of annual NOI before replacement reserves. The analyst tests $590,000 on the same pre-reserve basis after allowing for $60,000 more in recurring annual costs. On the proposed $6.0 million loan, assume scheduled annual principal and interest of $480,000. These are fictional figures; the $480,000 is a supplied payment assumption, not a rate quote. Under a simplified NOI-based convention, indicated debt service coverage is about **1.23×** on the adjusted basis ($590,000 ÷ $480,000), versus about **1.35×** on the reported basis. If annual debt service were $510,000, adjusted coverage would be about **1.16×**. For formal underwriting, document the loan structure, interest rate, amortization and any required stress assumptions. Confirm which cash-flow definition the lender uses and where it deducts replacement reserves. A lender's underwriting comparison can differ from the loan agreement's coverage covenant.

The requested loan is **66.7% of the contract price**. If the value used for this illustration were $8.4 million, the same loan would imply **71.4% loan-to-value (LTV)** on that basis. At this stage the $8.4 million is a sensitivity input. Formal sizing depends on the applicable value basis, policy limits and other relevant constraints, including the scope of debt counted. No number here is an eligibility rule, offer or commitment.

## Record what would move the request forward

- **Fit.** Confirm the lender considers this asset type, location, borrower and loan purpose. Stabilized, transitional and construction requests call for different evidence. An advertised multifamily program does not by itself establish eligibility.
- **Risk to investigate.** In this example, confirm the expense adjustments and check whether current leases and collections support the income before combining any revenue adjustment with the higher cost estimate. Verify available equity and whether the timing allows the necessary reports and decision.
- **Decision.** Advance to diligence if the request remains plausible with supportable assumptions. Ask for the missing information or a smaller request if the current numbers depend on a favorable assumption. Route to an appropriate alternative, or stop the screen, if there is no credible path.

Write down who owns the next action. The next analyst should know whether to reconcile income, verify sponsor capacity, ask for a revised capital plan or wait for a closing-date extension. The published T-12, rent roll, revenue underwriting and DSCR guides show how the early questions become documented financial and credit analysis.
